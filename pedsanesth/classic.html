Inhaled Nitric Oxide (iNO)
  • Dose in ppm, not by weight: start 20 ppm (usual 5–20 ppm). Higher doses add little benefit and raise toxicity.
  • Uses: pulmonary hypertensive crisis, PPHN, RV failure after cardiac surgery, hypoxemic respiratory failure.
  • Wean stepwise to 5 ppm, then 1 ppm, before stopping. Abrupt withdrawal can cause rebound pulmonary hypertension. Have a backup delivery system for transport.
  • Monitor methemoglobin (keep <5%) and NO₂ on the delivery device.
  • Caution: neonates dependent on right-to-left ductal shunting (e.g. HLHS) and LV dysfunction (may cause pulmonary edema).

Edit the dose or the syringe concentration to match your pump; the rate updates instantly. Standard concentrations vary by institution, so check every label.

Formulas used

• Weight estimate (APLS): 0–12 mo = 0.5×months + 4; 1–5 y = 2×age + 8; 6–12 y = 3×age + 7

• Cuffed ETT = age/4 + 3.5; uncuffed = age/4 + 4 (≥1 y), rounded down to 0.5 mm

• Oral ETT depth: ≥2 y = age/2 + 12; <2 y ≈ 3 × ETT ID; neonate ≈ weight(kg) + 6

• EBV: neonate 85, infant 80, child 75, adolescent 70 mL/kg

• BSA (Mosteller) = √(height cm × weight kg / 3600)

• IBW, ages 1–18: height <152.4 cm → height² × 1.65 / 1000; ≥152.4 cm → Devine (male 50 / female 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg per inch over 5 ft). Never exceeds TBW.

• ABW = IBW + 0.4 × (TBW − IBW)

• Obesity: BMI ≥ 95th percentile for age and sex (CDC 2000, ages 2–20), BMI ≥ 30 for adults. Not assessed under 2 years.
